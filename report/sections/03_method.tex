\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Baseline: Moonlight Muon}

For a weight matrix $W$ of size $m\times n$ with gradient $G_t$ at step $t$,
Moonlight Muon updates
\begin{align}
M_t &= 0.95M_{t-1}+G_t, \label{eq:momentum}\\
H_t &= G_t+0.95M_t, \label{eq:nesterov}\\
W_t &= (1-\eta_t\lambda)W_{t-1}
      -\eta_t\,0.2\sqrt{\max(m,n)}\operatorname{NS}_5(H_t). \label{eq:muon-step}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:momentum} keeps a running sum of past gradients (the
momentum), and \eqref{eq:nesterov} mixes it with the current gradient, a form
known as Nesterov momentum. $\operatorname{NS}_5$ approximates the nearest
orthogonal matrix to $H_t$: after dividing $H_t$ by its Frobenius norm, it
applies five steps of
\begin{equation}
X_{k+1}=aX_k+b\,(X_kX_k^\top)X_k+c\,(X_kX_k^\top)^2X_k,
\end{equation}
with $(a,b,c)=(3.4445,-4.7750,2.0315)$ \cite{jordan2024muon}. In
\eqref{eq:muon-step}, $\eta_t$ is the learning rate at step $t$, $\lambda$ is
the weight decay, and the factor $0.2\sqrt{\max(m,n)}$ rescales the update to
the typical size of an AdamW update \cite{liu2025muon}.

\subsection{Treatment: SOAP--Muon}

SOAP--Muon adds one step before the Newton--Schulz iteration. It rotates the
momentum into a coordinate system learned from past gradients, divides each
entry by a running estimate of its typical size (as Adam does), and then
applies Muon as before:
\begin{align}
C_t &= 0.95C_{t-1}+0.05\operatorname{Gram}(G_t), \\
\widehat H_t &= \operatorname{project}(H_t,Q_t), \\
V_t &= 0.95V_{t-1}+0.05(\widehat H_t\odot\widehat H_t), \\
U_t &= \operatorname{NS}_5\!\left(
 \widehat H_t\oslash\left(\sqrt{V_t/(1-0.95^t)}+10^{-8}\right)\right).
 \label{eq:soap-update}
\end{align}
Here $C_t$ is a running average of the gradient's covariance along the shorter
side of the matrix, and its eigenvectors $Q_t$ define the rotation;
$\operatorname{project}$ rotates $H_t$ into these coordinates. $V_t$ is a
running average of the squared rotated momentum, and \eqref{eq:soap-update}
divides by its square root, with a standard correction for the average's bias
early in training. The result $U_t$ is rotated back and applied with the same
scaling and weight decay as in \eqref{eq:muon-step}. To save computation, $Q_t$
is updated only every ten steps, using one power-iteration step followed by a
QR factorization.

The rotation alone would not change Muon's update, because the Newton--Schulz
iteration treats all rotated coordinate systems alike. The change comes from
dividing by $\sqrt{V_t}$, which gives each direction its own step size. The
design follows the one-sided SOAP variant \cite{vyas2025soap} and adapts public
reference code \cite{vyas_soapmuon_code}. It is not full SOAP, because the
rescaled update is still orthogonalized rather than applied directly as in
Adam.

\subsection{Which parameters use which optimizer}

In both optimizers, the embeddings (shared with the output layer) and the
layer-normalization parameters use AdamW, and the attention weight matrices use
Muon. The two optimizers differ only in the two MLP weight matrices of each
layer, which are 384 by 1,536: the baseline trains them with Muon and the
treatment with SOAP--Muon. Of the model's 33,664,128 parameters, AdamW trains
19,508,352 (58.0\%) and Muon trains the 4,718,592 attention-matrix parameters
(14.0\%); the remaining 9,437,184 MLP-matrix parameters (28.0\%) are the ones
whose optimizer changes. Table~\ref{tab:optimizer-definitions} lists all
settings.
