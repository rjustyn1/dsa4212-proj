Muon and SOAP are recent optimizers that exploit the matrix structure of
neural-network weights: Muon makes each weight update equally strong in all
directions, whereas SOAP rescales updates in coordinates learned from past
gradients. We test whether adding SOAP's rescaling step to Moonlight Muon, a
version of Muon for language models, improves the pretraining of a small
GPT-style model. After tuning the learning rate of both optimizers in the same
way, we train each on five new random seeds for 200M tokens. SOAP--Muon learns
faster at first, with a lower validation loss for every seed over the first 40M
tokens, but the lead disappears by about 80M tokens and becomes a small deficit.
As a result, our main measure, the average validation loss over training, shows
no reliable difference ($-0.034\%$), while SOAP--Muon ends with a slightly
higher loss for every seed and trains 2.37\% more slowly. An earlier comparison
against a less carefully tuned baseline had favored SOAP--Muon, which
illustrates how an under-tuned baseline can exaggerate the benefit of a new
method.
