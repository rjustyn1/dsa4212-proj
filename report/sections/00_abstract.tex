Muon applies a matrix-specific, Newton--Schulz-orthogonalized update to hidden
weights, whereas SOAP uses a Shampoo-derived preconditioner basis. We evaluate
whether one-sided SOAP preconditioning improves a Moonlight Muon baseline in a
controlled decoder-only Transformer pretraining setting. The treatment applies
the preconditioner only to MLP projection matrices and retains Muon or AdamW
for all other parameter groups. After route and learning-rate selection on a
separate screen, we compare the frozen treatment with the originally selected
unpreconditioned Moonlight Muon setting over five held-out paired seeds at a
nominal 200M-token budget.
The prespecified primary endpoint is token-normalised area under the
sampled validation negative-log-likelihood (NLL) curve. SOAP--Muon lowers this
area in all five pairs, with a mean paired difference of $-0.0060$
($-0.145\%$), but its mean NLL becomes slightly higher from 130M tokens onward. The
final full-validation NLL is mixed across seeds; SOAP--Muon is slower at the
shared NLL $\leq4.20$ wall-clock crossing in all five pairs, reduces training
throughput by 1.70\%, and increases peak memory by 0.055~GiB. However, a
subsequent matched shared-rate screen brackets both methods at
$4.8\times10^{-3}$ and gives SOAP--Muon the lower area at all three tested
rates. Because that screen uses one configuration-selection seed after the
original confirmation, the five-seed comparison remains an audit of the
initially selected setting rather than evidence of an optimizer improvement.
The shared-rate result motivates, but does not replace, a fresh held-out paired
confirmation.
