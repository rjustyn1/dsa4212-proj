Muon applies a matrix-specific, Newton--Schulz-orthogonalized update to hidden
weights, whereas SOAP uses a Shampoo-derived preconditioner basis. We evaluate
whether one-sided SOAP preconditioning improves a Moonlight Muon baseline in a
controlled decoder-only Transformer pretraining setting. The treatment applies
the preconditioner only to MLP projection matrices and retains Muon or AdamW
for all other parameter groups. A matched seed-101 screen selects a shared
$4.8\times10^{-3}$ learning rate for both methods. We then compare the frozen
configurations over five fresh held-out paired seeds at a nominal 200M-token
budget. The prespecified primary endpoint is token-normalised area under the
sampled validation negative-log-likelihood (NLL) curve. SOAP--Muon has lower
area in four of five pairs, but the mean paired difference is only $-0.0014$
($-0.034\%$) and its bootstrap 95\% interval crosses zero. SOAP--Muon leads in
every pair through 40M tokens, but the mean lead is gone by about 80M tokens.
SOAP--Muon has higher final full-validation NLL by $+0.0065$ on average,
reduces training throughput by 2.37\%, and increases peak memory by
0.055~GiB. Thus, at the screened shared rate, the added SOAP stage provides an
early lead but no reliable full-trajectory, final-quality, or wall-clock
advantage.
