\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Loss during training}

Fig.~\ref{fig:nll-difference}(a) shows that the two optimizers follow almost
the same loss curve, so the differences between them are small compared with
the overall progress of training. Fig.~\ref{fig:nll-difference}(b) magnifies
the difference. SOAP--Muon is ahead for every seed at each of the first four
measurements (10M--40M tokens). Its largest advantage, 0.0415 at 20M tokens,
means that Moonlight Muon needs about 1.5M more tokens, or 7\% more training,
to reach the same loss. The advantage then shrinks: the average difference
crosses zero at about 76M tokens, and from 120M tokens onward SOAP--Muon is
behind for every seed at every measurement.

Table~\ref{tab:confirmation-results} reports the main measure. SOAP--Muon has
the smaller curve area for four of the five seeds, but the mean difference is
only $-0.0014$, or 0.034\% of the baseline's value, and its 95\% confidence
interval, $[-0.0062,+0.0034]$, includes zero. Fig.~\ref{fig:nll-difference}(b)
shows why. The difference in area equals the shaded area divided by 200M
tokens: the early lead contributes $-0.0048$, but the later deficit gives back
$+0.0034$, so about 70\% of the early gain is lost by the end. The interval also
shows what the data rule out. Even the most favorable value consistent with the
data, a reduction of 0.0062, would be only 0.15\% of the baseline's value and
about one fifth of the 0.031 reduction that tuning the learning rate gave the
baseline (Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}). Any benefit over the whole run is
therefore small at best.

\subsection{Final loss}

At the end of training, SOAP--Muon has the higher loss for every seed
(Table~\ref{tab:confirmation-results}), with a mean difference of $+0.0065$ and
a 95\% confidence interval of $[+0.0022,+0.0107]$, which excludes zero. The gap
is small but not negligible: on the measurement curve, Moonlight Muon reached
SOAP--Muon's final loss about 13M tokens, or 6\% of training, before the end.
Both optimizers first reach a loss of 4.25 at 100M tokens for every seed, and a
loss of 4.20 at 120M tokens for four seeds; for seed 301, Moonlight Muon
reaches 4.20 one measurement earlier. The early lead therefore does not help
SOAP--Muon reach these loss levels sooner.

\subsection{Cost}

SOAP--Muon trains more slowly, at 96,236 instead of 98,570 tokens per second on
average ($-2.37\%$), and uses 0.055~GiB more peak GPU memory (11.509 instead of
11.454~GiB) for every seed (Table~\ref{tab:confirmation-systems}). The extra
memory is small, under 0.5\% of the total. Because SOAP--Muon never reaches a
loss level earlier and processes fewer tokens per second, it takes longer to
reach both levels for every seed (Table~\ref{tab:confirmation-wall-clock}). The
slowdown also shortens the early lead when elapsed time rather than tokens is
counted: at equal time, SOAP--Muon is ahead for every seed only up to about 20M
tokens, is behind on average from about 50M tokens, and is behind for every
seed from 90M tokens onward.

\subsection{How robust is the result?}
\label{sec:sensitivity}

Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity} reports two checks. The first is the earlier
five-seed comparison at the first-round learning rate of $1.2\times10^{-3}$
(seeds 201--205). It favored SOAP--Muon on the curve area for all five seeds,
with a confidence interval that excludes zero, but showed no consistent
difference in final loss. At the better-tuned rate, the baseline's average
curve area improved from 4.1562 to 4.1250 and its final loss from 3.9972 to
3.9702, while SOAP--Muon's advantage in curve area shrank by about three
quarters. In other words, much of SOAP--Muon's
earlier advantage came from a baseline that was not properly tuned. Because the
two experiments used different seeds, this comparison is descriptive.

The second check concerns the repeated run. The original A10G run for seed 305
had a final loss 0.0046 lower and a curve area 0.0047 lower than its A10
repeat, although both used the same seed, data, and settings. With the original
run, SOAP--Muon would be behind in final loss for four seeds instead of five,
and the confidence interval for the final-loss difference would include zero.
The overall conclusions are unchanged, but the statement that SOAP--Muon is
worse for every seed depends on the repeat.
