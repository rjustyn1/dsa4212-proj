\section{Discussion and Limitations}

The fresh confirmation records a narrow result: selective one-sided SOAP
preconditioning has lower sampled validation-NLL area in four of five pairs at
the matched shared rate. The mean effect is small and its bootstrap and
descriptive paired-$t$ intervals span zero. SOAP--Muon is lower in every pair
through 40M tokens, but the mean lead is gone by about 80M tokens. It also does
not translate into a final-NLL advantage: SOAP--Muon is higher on that endpoint
in every pair and on average. The fresh five-seed
result therefore does not support that SOAP preconditioning is better than a
properly screened Moonlight Muon baseline.

The early lead is also not free. SOAP--Muon has lower measured training
throughput, slightly higher peak device memory, and slower elapsed time at the
shared NLL $\leq4.20$ crossing in every pair. Within the 200M-token
runs, SOAP--Muon is lower in every pair through 40M tokens, and on the
40M-token screens it finished lower by 0.060 at $1.2\times10^{-3}$ and by
0.032 at $4.8\times10^{-3}$ on the screening seed. By 80M tokens the lead is
gone. This is consistent with a benefit confined to early training or short
schedules; with one screening seed, it remains a hypothesis rather than an
optimizer claim.

Several limitations bound even this descriptive interpretation. First, the model is a compact
8-layer Transformer trained on one fixed corpus regime and NVIDIA A10 GPUs. The
five seeds vary model initialization only: the deterministic token order is
held fixed, so their variation is conditional on that stream rather than a
sample of data-order variation. The result does not establish that the same
trade-off holds for larger models, other architectures, distributed training,
or longer token budgets. Second, the selected SOAP route and learning rate
came from a separate but single-seed screen. The initial Muon learning-rate
grid ended at its selected upper boundary, whereas the SOAP--Muon screen
varied only the routed MLP rate, holding AdamW and the attention matrices at
$1.2\times10^{-3}$. The later matched shared-rate screen repairs that
imbalance and brackets both methods at $4.8\times10^{-3}$, but it is only one
selection seed. The fresh seeds 301--305 were held out from that selection, so
they supply the relevant paired confirmation; nonetheless, the result is
limited to this model and fixed data order. Third, the 10M-token curve cadence
limits the resolution of the two secondary threshold metrics.

Finally, this experiment is designed to evaluate an outcome, not to prove a
mechanism. It does not contain a completed basis-age, covariance-spectrum, or
update-alignment diagnostic, nor does it compare every possible routing or
preconditioner-refresh choice. Consequently, the data do not justify an
attribution of the early lead to a particular spectral or curvature
effect. A future study should preregister such diagnostics or a fresh-seed
refresh-frequency ablation before using them to explain the early lead.
