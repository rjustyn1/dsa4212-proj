\section{Discussion and Limitations}

The confirmation study records a narrow result: selective one-sided SOAP
preconditioning lowers the sampled validation-NLL area relative to the
initially selected Moonlight Muon setting. The effect is small, occurs
primarily in the early part of training, and has the same direction in all five
paired area contrasts. The mean curve reverses slightly from 130M tokens
onward and does not translate into a reliable advantage in full-validation NLL
at 200M tokens. The post-confirmation learning-rate extension shows that the
initial baseline was under-tuned. The subsequent matched shared-rate screen
selects $4.8\times10^{-3}$ for both methods and favors SOAP--Muon at every
tested rate, but it uses a single selection seed. The five-seed result is
therefore not evidence that SOAP preconditioning is better than a properly
screened Moonlight Muon baseline.

The early difference is also not free. SOAP--Muon has lower measured training
throughput, slightly higher peak device memory, and slower elapsed time at the
shared NLL $\leq4.20$ crossing in all five pairs. The paper therefore treats
the observation as a token-indexed trajectory--systems-cost trade-off, rather
than an unconditional optimizer improvement. These data do not show that a
shorter cosine-decay schedule would reproduce the early curve advantage.

Several limitations bound even this descriptive interpretation. First, the model is a compact
8-layer Transformer trained on one fixed corpus regime and A10-class GPUs. The
five seeds vary model initialization only: the deterministic token order is
held fixed, so their variation is conditional on that stream rather than a
sample of data-order variation. The result does not establish that the same
trade-off holds for larger models, other architectures, distributed training,
or longer token budgets. Second, the selected SOAP route and learning rate
came from a separate but single-seed screen. The initial Muon learning-rate
grid ended at its selected upper boundary, whereas the treatment received more
route/learning-rate candidates. The later matched shared-rate screen repairs
that imbalance and brackets both methods at $4.8\times10^{-3}$, but it is only
one seed and was conducted after the original confirmation. Holding the five
confirmation seeds out prevents them from becoming tuning data; a fresh
held-out confirmation is still necessary. Third, the 10M-token curve cadence
limits the resolution of the two secondary threshold metrics.

Finally, this experiment is designed to evaluate an outcome, not to prove a
mechanism. It does not contain a completed basis-age, covariance-spectrum, or
update-alignment diagnostic, nor does it compare every possible routing or
preconditioner-refresh choice. Consequently, the data do not justify an
attribution of the trajectory benefit to a particular spectral or curvature
effect. A future study should preregister such diagnostics or a fresh-seed
refresh-frequency ablation before using them to explain the observed effect.
