\section{Introduction}

Training decoder-only Transformers is dominated by repeated matrix operations,
so an optimizer that changes the update geometry of hidden weight matrices can
affect both sample efficiency and systems cost \cite{vaswani2017attention}.
Muon applies a Newton--Schulz orthogonalization procedure to momentum updates
of hidden matrices, while leaving embeddings, output heads, and vector
parameters to an auxiliary optimizer \cite{jordan2024muon}. SOAP, in contrast,
uses a Shampoo-derived rotating basis to precondition adaptive updates
\cite{vyas2025soap}. These approaches suggest a direct question: can
SOAP-style preconditioning improve a Muon update without discarding Muon's
matrix-specific geometry?

We study this question in a controlled, small-scale Transformer pretraining
setting. Our primary comparison is unpreconditioned Moonlight Muon against a
selective one-sided SOAP--Muon treatment. The treatment applies smaller-side
SOAP preconditioning only to the MLP \texttt{up\_proj} and
\texttt{down\_proj} matrices; it retains Muon for the remaining eligible
hidden matrices and AdamW for the common auxiliary parameters. Learning rate,
weight decay, batch regime, data order, initialization seed, model, and token
budget are matched within each pair. The seed changes model initialization; the
frozen token stream and its order are shared by every run.

The configuration was selected on a separate short screen and then evaluated
on five held-out paired seeds at a nominal 200M-token budget. We use a
prespecified token-normalised area under the sampled validation-NLL curve as
the primary endpoint. This endpoint was fixed before confirmation, although an
earlier project proposal had foregrounded final NLL; both outcomes are
therefore reported. This design separates exploratory route selection from the
reported confirmation result and reports systems diagnostics alongside loss
metrics.

Across all five confirmation pairs, SOAP--Muon attains a lower primary
trajectory-area NLL than Moonlight Muon, with a mean paired difference of
$-0.0060$ ($-0.145\%$ relative to the baseline mean). The final 5M-token
validation NLL is mixed across seeds and differs by $+0.0009$ on average,
while SOAP--Muon trains 1.70\% slower and uses 0.055~GiB more peak training
memory. The trajectory-area contrast is concentrated before 100M tokens and
reverses slightly in the late mean curve. Thus, the evidence supports an early,
token-indexed trajectory difference in this setting, not a final-quality or
wall-clock improvement.

The contribution of this paper is threefold. First, it provides a reproducible
paired evaluation of SOAP preconditioning on top of a Moonlight Muon baseline.
Second, it isolates the preconditioning change through fixed routing and
matched seed/data controls. Third, it reports the sampled trajectory, endpoint,
wall-clock, throughput, and memory trade-offs, including the cases where the
treatment does not improve final NLL. Our conclusions are restricted to the
implemented architecture, data regime, and 200M-token budget.
