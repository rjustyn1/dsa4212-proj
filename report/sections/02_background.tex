\section{Background and Related Work}

Our experiments use a compact decoder-only Transformer, an architecture built
from attention and feed-forward matrix operations \cite{vaswani2017attention}.
For such models, optimizer choice affects not only the number of updates needed
to reach a loss level but also the memory and wall-clock cost of each update.
Adam estimates diagonal first and second moments of the gradient
\cite{kingma2015adam}; AdamW decouples weight decay from the adaptive update
\cite{loshchilov2019adamw}. These methods provide the auxiliary optimizer for
parameter types that do not fit the matrix-specific updates studied here.

Shampoo is a structure-aware preconditioning method that maintains
preconditioners along tensor dimensions, allowing matrix geometry to enter the
update while avoiding a dense preconditioner over every parameter
\cite{gupta2018shampoo}. Its additional state and matrix operations motivate
methods that retain useful preconditioning while controlling systems cost.
SOAP interprets Shampoo through a rotating preconditioner eigenbasis and
combines that basis with Adam-style moment estimates
\cite{vyas2025soap}. The SOAP study also reports one-sided variants that
reduce the cost of preconditioning a rectangular matrix by retaining the basis
on only its smaller side \cite{vyas2025soap}.

Muon instead targets hidden weight matrices, using momentum followed by a
Newton--Schulz approximation to a semi-orthogonal update. Its official
specification assigns embeddings, output heads, and hidden gains or biases to
an auxiliary AdamW optimizer \cite{jordan2024muon}. That specification is a
technical note and reference implementation, rather than a peer-reviewed
paper; we cite it as the source of the optimizer definition used in this
study. The subsequent Moonlight scaling study identifies decoupled weight
decay and per-parameter update scaling as important when extending Muon to
large language-model training \cite{liu2025muon}; these are consequently
explicit, frozen parts of the baseline defined in Section~III.

This work asks a narrow empirical question at a fixed training budget: whether
adding one-sided SOAP preconditioning to a Muon-aligned update on selected MLP
projection matrices improves the validation-loss trajectory relative to an
unpreconditioned Moonlight Muon baseline. It does not claim to reproduce the
full SOAP configuration or to establish a new optimizer family. The controlled
comparison instead isolates the practical value of this preconditioning choice
under the stated model, data, and systems constraints.
